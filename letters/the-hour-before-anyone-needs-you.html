The Hour Before Anyone Needs You

There's an hour most houses have that most people never meet.

It comes before the alarms and before the first question of the day gets asked. The windows are still gray, and the floor is cold enough that you notice where you step.

Nothing in this hour is urgent. That's the whole point of it.

You fill the kettle and wait. You don't check your phone, because the phone belongs to the other hours. Here there's only the tick of the heating element, the refrigerator's low hum, and the small sound of your own breathing, which you forgot you could hear.

When the water's ready, you pour it into the heavy mug, the one with thick walls that holds heat longer than you need it to. That matters more than it sounds like it should. A thin cup cools fast, and a cooling cup rushes you. This one lets you sit, so you do.

Twenty minutes, maybe less. Then the house wakes up and the hour is gone.

But you met it. And the day that follows feels slightly different, like something has already gone right.
